# Australian Rental Pressure Intelligence

## Project Overview

This project investigates rental pressure across Australia using publicly available demographic, housing-market and economic data.

The analysis will examine how rental pressure varies across regions and over time, and whether it is statistically associated with factors such as population growth, housing supply, rental affordability and broader economic conditions.

## Research Question

What demographic, housing-supply and economic factors are associated with rental pressure across Australian regions, and how have these relationships changed over time?

## Project Objectives

1. Analyse changes in Australian rental prices over time.
2. Examine population growth and demographic changes across regions.
3. Analyse housing supply using dwelling approvals and related indicators.
4. Examine rental affordability and rental stress.
5. Compare rental pressure between capital cities, regional areas and states/territories.
6. Investigate statistical associations between rental pressure and potential explanatory variables.
7. Develop a transparent Rental Pressure Index for analytical purposes.
8. Build machine-learning models to investigate which variables are useful for predicting rental pressure.
9. Develop an interactive dashboard presenting the findings.
10. Produce a reproducible technical report documenting the methodology, results and limitations.

## Analytical Scope

The project will investigate:

- Rental prices and rental growth
- Population and population growth
- Housing supply and dwelling approvals
- Rental affordability and rental stress
- Income where suitable data is available
- Interest rates and broader economic conditions
- Geographic differences
- Relationships between rental pressure and potential explanatory variables

## Rental Pressure Index

A custom Rental Pressure Index will be developed using selected indicators of rental-market pressure.

The index is a project-created analytical measure and is not an official Australian government statistic.

Different weighting and normalization approaches will be tested to examine the sensitivity of the results.

## Statistical Analysis

Potential methods include:

- Descriptive statistics
- Correlation analysis
- Time-series analysis
- Regional comparisons
- Linear regression
- Feature importance analysis
- Sensitivity analysis

## Machine Learning

Potential models include:

- Linear Regression
- Random Forest
- Gradient Boosting

Models will be evaluated against appropriate baseline models and interpreted rather than treated as purely predictive systems.

Initial candidate sources include:

- Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS)

Final datasets will be selected after evaluating geographic coverage, time coverage, data quality, licensing and compatibility.

## Important Limitation

This is an observational data-analysis project.

The project will investigate statistical associations rather than claim that individual variables cause changes in rental pressure.

The project does not attempt to evaluate government housing policy or establish causal relationships.
